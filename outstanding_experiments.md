# Outstanding experiment ideas (as of 2026-11-04)

Context for a fresh chat: the STS Cockpit FNN predicts Heart-kill probability from deck, relics, floor and character (929 input features). The v21 pipeline uses a 5-head multi-target network (act 1 boss, act 2 boss, act 3 first boss, act 3 second boss at A20 only, Heart kill) with a deterministic `md5(play_id) % 5 == 0` test split. Labels of -1 are ignored in the loss. The Heart head is last. The goal is a model that helps players choose cards and build decks, so correctly capturing card and relic interactions matters, and a small accuracy loss is acceptable in exchange for better interactions.

## Ideas

### Interactions between cards (priority)

1. **Explicit pairwise branch.** Add a factorization-machine-style term, or a small pairwise network, on top of the main network, trained jointly. This is the most direct fix for stacked combos the models miss. It is also where the accuracy-for-interactions trade-off could appear. Evaluate with the combo and card-pick checks below, not log loss alone.
2. **Cumulative upgrade encoding (v22). Done 2026-11-02.** `upgrade_encoding=cumulative` gives a small, consistent gain on every recipe: log loss 0.0003 lower (0.00025-0.00035), AUC +0.0005-0.001, average precision +0.004-0.005, Brier and F1 better, precision/recall/F1 at 0.5 similar or slightly better. It makes rare upgrade states borrow from the plain card: for pairs with a strong interaction, the rarest upgrade state now keeps 106-116% of the interaction seen in the best-supported state (v21: 55-68%). It did not fix the negative upgrade effects (share of cards where upgrading looks harmful: 12-18% before and after, slightly worse for the beta and alpha+beta models), and combo residuals are unchanged (Barricade x Entrench train residual 0.41 to 0.36 multi-target, 0.46 to 0.44 single target; Barricade + Entrench + Body Slam and Corruption + Dead Branch still undervalued). Keep it as the default for new models. Reports: `model_objects/bench/v22_*/`.
3. **Combo-targeted loss or data weighting.** Upweight rows that contain known combos, or build synthetic pairs. Untested guess: the loss gain from any single combo is too small to matter in training.

11. **Prior that upgrading never hurts. Done 2026-11-04: synthetic flip is the default.** In the v22 bench 12-18% of well-supported cards and 33-36% of rare-state cards have a negative model-implied upgrade effect (e.g. Lessons Learned -0.54, Metamorphosis -0.30, Unload -0.22), though nearly every upgrade is strictly better in the game. Hypothesis (user's): the model confounds upgrade choices with card quality. Players upgrade the wrong card, or upgrade it in runs that are already struggling or already doomed, so the upgraded copy's column soaks up that selection effect and reads as "this upgraded card is worse". Ways to impose the prior: (a) cumulative encoding with a non-negativity constraint on the `card_u` weights' effect (e.g. softplus on the `card_u` contribution, or a hinge penalty when the model's upgrade effect on a held-out batch goes below zero); (b) a regularizer that pulls `card_u` weights toward zero so the upgraded copy defaults to the plain card (cumulative encoding already does this in effect, but nothing pushes the sign); (c) a monotonic penalty computed on training batches by flipping each upgraded card to plain and requiring the logit to drop. Evaluate: share of cards with negative upgrade effect (target ~0), held-out log loss (expect a small loss if the confounding is real and the model was using it), and calibration of rare upgrade states. Also check whether the negative effects concentrate in cards that are mostly upgraded late or in struggling runs: compare floor and prior-win-rate of runs that upgrade each flagged card. Uses `upgrade_effects.csv` from the bench.
12. **Search the data for confounders.** Look for variables outside the deck itself that explain both which cards get chosen or upgraded and whether the run wins, so they can be added as inputs, used for stratification, or used to reweight rows. The aim is to remove spurious correlations the model learns, such as "upgrading this card is bad" (item 11) or card preferences that differ by act (item 10). Candidates to check, and what the data currently has:
    - **Player id.** The raw run events may carry a player identifier; so far only `play_id` (a run id, used for the hash split) is used. Strong players both win more and make different card and upgrade choices (they skip cards, upgrade selectively), so card effects absorb player skill. Check whether a player id exists, how many runs per player, and how much of the win rate is explained by player alone (a player-only baseline's log loss and AUC).
    - **Run metadata:** ascension level (already pooled across ascensions, so check it is an input or at least stratified on), game version and date (a card's strength changes between patches; the data is filtered to runs after 2020-07-01, `timestamp` at `scripts/process_data.py:156`), chosen seed (excluded), and whether the run is a daily or custom mode run.
    - **Upgrade source and timing:** whether a card was upgraded at a campfire, by an event, or from a relic (Whetstone, War Paint), the floor it was upgraded at, and what else the player could have done at that campfire (rest, lift, dig). Upgrading while at low HP instead of resting, or upgrading late in an already-doomed run, would read as "upgrading hurts".
    - **State not in the 929 features:** current and max HP, gold, potions, and Ruby key. These drive both choices and survival and are absent from the input vector.
    - **Survivorship by floor:** which rows each upgrade state appears in (deck size and floor distribution of plain versus upgraded copies of the same card).
    Method: for each candidate, (a) check whether it differs between plain-card and upgraded-card rows for the cards flagged in item 11, (b) re-fit the offset regression from the bench with the candidate as a control and see how much the upgrade effect moves, and (c) retrain with the candidate as an input or as a stratification weight and compare the upgrade effects, held-out log loss and calibration. If a confounder explains the negative effects, adding it as an input (and setting it to a neutral value at the cockpit's prediction time) is the cleanest fix, and it would make item 11's constraint unnecessary.

Max HP above 200 had a 54% Heart rate against about 3% overall. `is_modded()` in `process_data.py` now sends these runs from finetuning and beta to alpha pretraining. **The existing `.bin` files still contain them and v22/v23 were trained on them**, so the next data rebuild changes the test set slightly (see comparison note in item 14).
- *Does controlling for a candidate move the upgrade effect?* Mantel-Haenszel log odds ratio of upgraded vs plain, train rows, 29 flagged cards (model upgrade effect below -0.05, 1,000+ rows in both states). Baseline strata are floor x character x deck size x upgrade count x relic count:

### Inputs

13. **Potions as inputs. Investigated 2026-11-03, not used: the data cannot observe potion inventory.** `potions_obtained` has the name and floor of every pickup (42 potion types once the spelling is normalized: "Elixir" and "ElixirPotion" are the same potion), but `potions_floor_usage` has only the floors potions were used on, no names, and nothing else in the run record (combats, events, shops, campfires, cards) names a used or discarded potion. So the held inventory has to be reconstructed by matching uses to pickups. Measured on 910 sampled A20 rows (small sample: an earlier 224 row pass gave 73% instead of 82%, so read it as roughly 75-85%): 71% of rows hold at least one potion; 82% of those have an exactly determined inventory (ambiguous only when two or more different potions are held and one is used; median ambiguous row has about 3 possible inventories, 95th percentile 5); 17.5% of potion-holding rows imply more potions than the slots allow (3, or 5 with Potion Belt), which means unlogged discards, so the reconstruction overstates what was held; 2.7% of rows have a use with nothing logged as held (potions from unlogged sources such as Entropic Brew and Alchemize). Considered encodings: expected count per potion over all matchings consistent with the log (42 columns), or a few grouped columns. **Decision (user, 2026-11-03): the potion data is too unreliable, drop potions for now.** Revisit only if the game's own run history is found to record held potions (nothing in this dump does). HP, max HP and gold are logged per floor in every era and are used (item 14).
14. **HP and gold as input features (v24). Done 2026-11-04. Decided 2026-11-03, to include; potions dropped (item 13).** Stage 1 of item 12 showed HP and gold carry real signal (logistic baseline test log loss 0.0683 to 0.0674 with HP fraction and 0.0677 with gold; all candidates together 0.0646) that the 929 features miss, even though they do not explain the negative upgrade effects. Plan: add current HP fraction, max HP and gold as input columns, run finetune-only multi head variants (+HP only, +gold only, +HP and gold) and the full alpha + beta pretraining for +HP and gold, all on the v23 recipe (cumulative encoding, flip weight 1), data rebuilt into `data/training_data_v24` with modded runs moved to alpha. Baselines are v22/v23 re-evaluated on the cleaned v24 test set (they were trained on the modded runs); one seed each, seed noise about 0.0002 log loss. Queue and results: see "v24 results" below.

15. **Incorporate the Neow bonus (added 2026-11-06).** The raw runs log `neow_bonus` (e.g. THREE_ENEMY_KILL, BOSS_RELIC, RANDOM_COMMON_RELIC, TEN_PERCENT_HP_BONUS, ONE_RARE_RELIC, THREE_RARE_CARDS, TRANSFORM_TWO_CARDS, REMOVE_TWO, UPGRADE_CARD, plus colorless, potion and gold options) and `neow_cost` (NONE, NO_GOLD, PERCENT_DAMAGE, TEN_PERCENT_HP_LOSS, CURSE). Today only the deck effects reach the model: `run_replay.py` applies the removals, transforms, upgrades and curse of the bonus (`NEOW_BONUS`) and the relic or cards it gives end up in the deck and relic columns, but the bonus choice itself and its cost are not an input. Open questions, in order: (a) does a Neow one-hot (bonus and cost, about 20 and 5 columns) improve held-out log loss on top of the deck state, or is it already implied by the deck (its value is the same as item 14's test: add it as an input and compare against the matched model with the same recipe); (b) the bonuses with no deck footprint (THREE_ENEMY_KILL, which is the most common A20 choice, HP bonus, gold, potions) carry the information the model currently lacks, so they are where any gain would show; (c) which Neow bonus to take is a decision for the simulation tool, so it needs the offered options (not logged: only the picked bonus and its cost are), which limits it to a model-based ranking of the bonuses by predicted win probability, with the selection caveats of item 12; (d) check how the bonus relates to the player's later choices before adding it (it may act as a skill proxy, e.g. stronger players taking riskier bonuses), as in item 12. Needs a data rebuild (`process_data.py` writes the input columns), so it should be bundled with any other input change, and it changes the input layout for the UI (`ui/server/engine.py`). Not started.

16. **Input the observed act boss from the map (added 2026-11-06).** In the game the boss of each act is visible on the map from the start of the act, and which boss it is changes what a deck needs (Guardian and Hexaghost reward front-loaded damage and block, Champ and Collector reward sustained scaling, Time Eater punishes many cheap cards, Awakened One punishes powers, and so on). The model never sees it, so the act 1 head in particular averages over three bosses (and the act 2 and 3 heads over three each). Adding the boss of the current act as an input (one-hot, three to four per act, with an "unknown" value) lets the heads condition on it and lets the simulation tool score a pick against the boss the player will actually fight. Open issues: (a) **the boss is not logged directly.** The raw runs carry it only through the boss fight itself (a `damage_taken` entry on floor 16, 33 or 50 with the enemy name, and `killed_by`) so it is known only for runs that reached the fight; runs that died earlier have it missing, and missing is correlated with the label. Training with the boss as an input only where it is known would leak: "boss known" means "survived to floor 16". Check `path_per_floor` and `path_taken` first for a map or boss field; if there is none, the boss has to be imputed or the input has to be masked in a way that carries no label information (for example always drop it for floors after the fight is logged and train on rows with it and rows with "unknown" at the same rate regardless of outcome), or the data cannot support this item. (b) Floors 1-16 have the same boss for the whole act, so it is a run-level covariate: check that it is not a proxy for run seed or game version, and whether the boss pool changed with patches. (c) Evaluate with the per-head log loss, especially `beat_act1_boss`, and with the bench: the card picks and counterfactual effects should now differ by boss (a good sign: Barricade and Corruption effects against Guardian versus Hexaghost). (d) The input layout and the UI change (a boss selector per act). Not started; may need a data rebuild, so bundle with item 15 and any other input change.

17. **Calibration of boss relic effects (added 2026-11-06).** Found in the UI (Ironclad, floor 17, HP 15/75, 214 gold, six relics, 17 cards, `fnn_v25_alpha_beta_mt_hp_gold_flip_synthetic`). Scoring one relic at a time against "no boss relic" gave swings that look too large and too uneven: Snecko Eye +13.7 pts Heart (blend +33%), Fusion Hammer +5.8, Cursed Key +2.2, Busted Crown -1.0, Sozu -5.5, Runic Dome -4.8, Sacred Bark -9.9, Black Star -11.7, Astrolabe -15.7 (base Heart 19.3%). The weak relics (Sozu, Runic Dome) being negative is believable; the size and the spread are not, and the three offered at the act 1 boss all came out below "skip". Suspected causes, to test in this order: (a) **the baseline is off distribution.** `run_replay.py` gives a boss relic floor 17, 34 or 51 (`BOSS_RELIC_FLOORS`), so a row at floor 17 or later nearly always holds one; "no boss relic at floor 17" is a state the model has almost never seen (skipped picks only). Count rows at floors 17+ with no boss relic and what their outcomes are; if few, every relic's delta against "none" is measured against a rare, probably unrepresentative, state. (b) **selection and skill.** Which boss relic a player takes, and with what deck, is chosen; Snecko Eye or Fusion Hammer takers may be a different kind of run (item 12). The offered set is logged (`boss_relics[].picked` and `not_picked`, already read by `scripts/extract_relic_decisions.py`), so the check can be done properly: among runs offered the same three, compare outcomes by pick, adjusted for deck, HP, gold and ascension, and compare with the model's predicted ordering of those three. (c) **per relic calibration.** For each boss relic: predicted against observed rate on held-out rows that hold it, by floor band, with counts and an interval (ECE per relic, log loss by relic), and a matched check: rows holding the relic against rows that differ only by that relic. Small counts should be shrunk or flagged. (d) **stability.** Compare the same relic deltas across v22, v24 and v25: effects that swing between models are noise, not signal. (e) **relics whose value is in the deck.** Astrolabe, Empty Cage, Pandora's Box and Calling Bell are scored as the relic alone in the plain relic list (the planner in the Relic tab handles the first three), and Black Blood, Ring of the Serpent style swaps replace the starter relic, but the tool adds them on top of it. Calling Bell (a curse and three relics) is not modelled. Possible fixes depending on the findings: a prior or shrinkage on relic effects, training with the pairwise branch (item 1), a held-out relic choice test (does the model pick what players pick, and do the picks that disagree do worse), or dropping relic numbers from the tool when they cannot be shown to be calibrated. UI follow-up regardless of the result: rank a boss relic offer against the other offered relics rather than against skipping (skip is rare), model swaps (remove the replaced relic), and mark relic numbers as the least reliable output. Not started.

18. **Finetune on every ascension with ascension as an input (added 2026-11-06).** Today the data is staged by ascension: finetuning is A20 only (post-Watcher, not beta, not modded, about 1.5M runs), beta pretraining is the same filter at A0 to A19 (`process_data.py` moves a finetuning run below A20 to `pre_training_beta`), and alpha pretraining is everything else. The ascension is never an input, so the beta stage teaches the network lower-ascension outcomes with no way to tell them apart from A20, and the finetune stage then corrects that on A20 alone. `process_data.py` already lists "group pretrain beta with finetune by adding ascension column" as a TODO. The question is whether one finetuning set with all ascensions and an ascension input beats the staged recipe on the target (A20) held-out set, which is what the tool is used for. Reasons it could help: much more finetuning data per card and combo (the A20 set is the scarce part, and the pair and triple effects of items 1 and 3 are data limited), no train and deploy mismatch from the beta stage, and the network can learn what changes with difficulty (enemy health and damage, elites, the double boss at A20) instead of averaging it. Reasons it might not: lower ascension runs are easier and played by lower skill players, so ascension also carries a skill proxy (see item 12), and pooled rows could teach card values that are true at A5 but not at A20 if the ascension interactions are not learned; the second boss head is A20 only (label -1 below), so it only trains on a fraction of the rows; training time grows with the data (finetuning has to fit in RAM to be shuffled). Design: (a) count rows and runs per ascension in the finetuning-quality data (A0 to A20) before building anything; (b) input as a scalar `ascension / 20`, plus an `A20` flag or a one-hot of coarse bands (0-9, 10-14, 15-19, 20) so the network can give A20 its own behaviour, and compare the encodings; (c) train on all ascensions from scratch, and as the existing recipe (alpha, then beta plus finetune merged with the ascension input); (d) variants: only A15 and above, and A20 rows upweighted; (e) evaluate on the unchanged A20 held-out set (`md5(play_id) % 5 == 0`, modded runs removed) and report log loss, AUC, ECE, Brier and precision, recall and F1 at 0.5, per head, then the card pick bench (`extract_pick_decisions.py` on A20 held-out runs), the combo and upgrade benches (item 11, negative upgrade effects) and the boss relic calibration of item 17; (f) check the learned ascension effect directly: score the same decks at A0, A10, A15 and A20 and make sure the win probability falls monotonically and the card effects keep their order. A model with the ascension input also needs the choice in the UI (default 20) and a new input layout (`ui/server/engine.py`, a new `extra_features` group like `hp` and `gold`), so it should be built together with the other input changes (items 14 to 16 and the Neow bonus) in one data rebuild. Not started.

### Faster training: cheaper flip, larger batch, more epochs (2026-11-05)

All finetune-only, multi head, v25 recipe, one seed each (seed noise about 0.0002 log loss), numbers in the table above. Baseline is `fnn_v25_ft_mt_hp_gold_flip_synthetic` (batch 64, flip every step, 20 epochs): 0.05239 log loss, 134 min.

- *Cheaper flip, batch 64.* `upgrade_flip_every=4` (penalty every 4th batch, weight x4): 0.05243 log loss, 0.9256 AUC, 71 min. Fewer flip rows (8+8 instead of 32+32): 0.05242, 123 min. Both are within noise of the baseline; `every4` halves the time, fewer rows barely helps. Zero negative upgrade effects out of 344 cards for both (bench `flip_cost`).
- *Batch 256 (lr 0.12, linear scaling), 20 epochs.* `every4_b256` 0.05265 / 0.9250 in 27 min (5x faster than baseline, +0.0003 log loss); `rows8_b256` 0.05270 in 42 min. ECE is the best of any finetune-only model (0.00028 and 0.00025). Zero negative upgrade effects for both (`flip_cost_b256`).
- *Learning rate at batch 256.* lr 0.24: 0.05258 (inside noise of 0.12); lr 0.06: 0.05312 (worse by 0.0005). 0.12 was about right.
- *Epochs are the lever.* `every4_b256_ep40` (40 finetune epochs, lr 0.12): **0.05192 log loss, 0.9273 AUC, 0.4859 avg precision, ECE 0.00019, Brier 0.012331, P / R / F1 at 0.5 0.707 / 0.303 / 0.424, 53 min.** Better than the batch-64 baseline on every metric at 40% of its time. 60 epochs (`_ep60`): 0.05194, 0.9271, 0.4868, 83 min, so the gain has flattened by 40; use 40. Caveat: the baseline only had 20 epochs; batch 64 at 40 epochs was not run, so this shows batch 256 is not capped below batch 64, not that it is better at equal epochs. Bench (`flip_tuning`): zero negative upgrade effects for lr 0.24, lr 0.06 and ep40.
- *Without HP and gold (the option to track them or not).* Same recipe with `extra_features=[]`: batch 64, 20 epochs `fnn_v25_ft_mt_flip_synthetic` 0.05336 / 0.9215 (72 min); batch 256, 20 epochs `_b256` 0.05360 / 0.9207 (27 min); batch 256, 40 epochs `fnn_v25_ft_mt_flip_synthetic_b256_ep40` **0.05301 / 0.9229, 0.4707 avg precision, ECE 0.00033, P / R / F1 0.698 / 0.292 / 0.412, 54 min**. The reference with no extras, `fnn_v22_ft_mt_cum_flip1`, is 0.05411 / 0.9222, so the no-extras model is 0.0011 better on log loss, with AUC about equal; it needs no HP or gold at inference. Cost of dropping the inputs at equal settings: 0.0011 log loss, 0.004 AUC (matches the earlier 0.001 estimate). Zero negative upgrade effects for both no-extras models (`flip_tuning`).
- *Pretraining with the fast recipe (2026-11-06).* `fnn_v25_alpha_beta_mt_hp_gold_flip_every4_b256_ep40` (batch 256, lr 0.12, flip every 4th step in every stage, alpha 4 / beta 10 / finetune 40 epochs, the same pretraining as the best model): 0.05194 log loss, 0.9272 AUC, avg precision 0.4864, ECE 0.00066, Brier 0.012330, P / R / F1 at 0.5 0.714 / 0.296 / 0.419, 425 min, zero negative upgrade effects (`pretrained_fast`). **It is no better than finetune-only at the same settings** (0.05192, 53 min) and its calibration is worse (ECE 0.00066 vs 0.00019); the pretrained batch-64 model stays best (0.05137, 0.9292, 724 min). So pretraining only pays off here when the pretraining stages keep batch 64 and the flip every step. Untested causes: batch 256 / lr 0.12 in the alpha and beta stages, or the flip every 4th step during pretraining (the finetune stage was tested alone). The pretrained no-HP/gold counterpart was dropped by the user on 2026-11-06 (no longer needed: HP and gold were first added as upgrade confounders, which was solved another way, and the accuracy gain is about 0.001) and so was the two-model pick agreement on the pretrained pair; the finetune-only pair stands in for it (83% same best card, 90% same best boss relic on a 4,725-decision preview, 97% same top choice where both see a clear winner; `model_objects/bench/agreement_ft_test`; the section is `sections=agreement` in `scripts/interaction_bench.py`, relic offers from `scripts/extract_relic_decisions.py`, 32,811 decisions).
- *Recommended fast recipe:* `batch_size=256 lr=0.12 upgrade_flip_every=4`, 40 finetune epochs, finetune only (about 53 min versus 134). For the highest accuracy use the pretrained batch-64 best model (724 min). Not yet done: pretraining with batch 64 for alpha and beta and batch 256 only for finetuning (untested split of the recipe), a 40-epoch batch-64 control, combos and picks on the fast models, the agreement test on the full relic set. Incident: the first batch-64 upgrade bench in the queue failed on an unset variable and was rerun by hand; results above are from the rerun. Another: the first pretrained run was started with default epochs (8 / 20) by mistake, about twice the baseline's pretraining, and restarted with 4 / 10 after about 1.5 h.

## Suggested order

Done: items 2 (cumulative encoding), 4 (head weights: keep act 1), 10 (act 1 head disagreement), 11 (upgrade prior: synthetic flip is the default), 14 (HP and gold; best model `fnn_v24_alpha_beta_mt_hp_gold`, needs real HP and gold at inference); item 12 stage 1 (confounders do not explain negative upgrade effects); item 13 closed (potions not observable); item 7 dropped. Open, in rough order: 1 (explicit pairwise branch: the lever for three-card combos, which every head undervalues), then 8 (shorter alpha schedule), 3, 9, 5, 15 (Neow bonus input), 16 (act boss input), and the rest of item 12. Build new experiments on the v25 recipe (new defaults, best model `fnn_v25_alpha_beta_mt_hp_gold_flip_synthetic`). Speed (2026-11-05, finetune-only, 400k-row epochs, MPS, `rows_per_second` in metrics.csv): reading batches ahead in a thread (`prefetch_batches=8`, built, default 0) gives no speedup (13.2k vs 13.1-13.5k rows/s, training losses bit-identical), so the disk read is not the bottleneck. The synthetic flip penalty is: without the flip 36.7k rows/s, with it 13.2k (about 2.8x slower per step; the 47% extra seen earlier was inflated by other load). ## How to compare

- Heart-head log loss, AUC and average precision on the hash-split held-out set (see the table above), plus the per-segment breakdown in each model's `segments.csv`.
- Counterfactual pair and triple interactions on curated combos (Barricade + Entrench, Barricade + Body Slam, Corruption + Feel No Pain, Catalyst + Noxious Fumes, Corruption + Dead Branch, Barricade + Entrench + Body Slam).
- Observed vs expected wins on decks containing each combo, with run-clustered z-scores.
- Card-pick calibration (per-card observed vs expected over 173 cards with 1,000+ picks) and agreement with Heart-killers' picks.
- Do not run two experiments at once with `log_experiment=true`.
